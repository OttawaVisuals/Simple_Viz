06Text — title formula

The catalogue currently mixes all three of these. Same page, three headline registers.

X-1 · question
How deep is the well?

Names the thing the reader wants to know. Reads as a puzzle; the page is the answer.

X-2 · the surprise, stated
The splash makes two journeys

Leads with the counter-intuitive fact itself. Tells the reader what they are about to learn rather than what they are about to be asked.

X-3 · wry
Why the stone lies about the depth

Gives the physics a character. Warmer, and closest to Why the straw gives up — but it can drift into cute if all 63 pages do it.

07Text — subtitle length

There is no cap today, and it shows: subtitles run from 22 words to over 60. Same page, three lengths.

S-25 · 25 words, one sentence

Drop a stone and start a timer. The splash you hear has made two journeys: the stone fell down, then its sound climbed back up.

S-40 · 40 words, two sentences, carries a real number

Drop a stone and start a timer. The splash has made two journeys — the stone falling under gravity, then its sound climbing back at 343 m/s — and ignoring the second one makes a 60 m well look like 78 m.

S-60 · 60+ words, the current long end

Drop a stone down a well and start a timer. The splash you hear has made two journeys: first the stone falls under gravity, accelerating the whole way, and then the sound of the impact climbs back up the shaft at a constant 343 m/s. Most people time the whole thing and treat it as pure free fall, which is why a 60 m well gets reported as 78 m.

CLAUDE.md already asks for one subtitle paragraph that states the real numbers and the concept in one breath — S-40 is the closest reading of that. A word cap is what makes it enforceable.

08Text — voice

Three smaller calls that need a ruling rather than a specimen. Answer inline and I will write them in.

V-PERSON

Second person and imperative (“Drop a stone and start a timer”) versus neutral (“A stone is dropped”). The site currently leans second-person throughout — confirm that as the rule?

V-UNITS

Metric first with imperial in brackets where a reader would think in imperial — 60 m (200 ft) — or metric only? Note that pizza-area is inches-native because that is how pizza is sold, so the rule needs an “unless the object is sold in imperial” clause either way.

V-JARGON

May a title use the technical name unglossed (Reynolds number, Snell’s law), or must the title be plain language, with the technical name appearing only in the topbar tag and the closing note?

Spelling is already settled: Canadian — metre, colour, centre, neighbour.
