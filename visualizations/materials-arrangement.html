Why materials are not all arranged alike

Copper, brass, steel, ceramic, plastic and glass may all look solid — but their atoms organise in fundamentally different ways.

These are teaching diagrams, not photographs of a specific sample. Copper and aluminium are face-centred-cubic metals; brass and bronze are substitutional copper alloys; mild steel has iron-rich phases plus small carbon atoms in interstitial positions; stainless steels have several alloying elements; alumina is an ionic/covalent ceramic; polyethylene and nylon are long-chain polymers; and ordinary glass is amorphous rather than crystalline. The shell diagrams show neutral-atom electron shells, not the bonding or ion charge inside the material. Real materials change with composition, temperature, processing and cooling rate — there is no one universal atomic picture of a broad material class.
