Why the straw gives up.

Push a fluid through a tube and it fights back with viscous drag — enough that on Taskmaster, Tim Vine couldn't blow a candle out through drinking straws taped end to end, while a garden hose of similar length worked fine. Here's the equation behind that.

Hagen–Poiseuille law for slow, steady (laminar) flow through a round tube, where η is viscosity, L is length, r is inner radius (half the diameter set above), and Q is a fixed, representative flow rate. The scale's limits are rough estimates of what a person can sustain by mouth, not hard numbers. This models horizontal flow only — a straw held upright also fights gravity, and no amount of suction can lift water more than about 10 m, since that alone needs a full atmosphere of pressure difference. Real chained drinking straws also kink at their joints, choking flow by a mechanism this equation doesn't capture — likely a bigger factor in Tim Vine's attempt than viscosity alone.
