Fan affinity laws

A little slower saves a lot of fan power

Slow a fan by 20% and airflow falls by 20% — but the ideal shaft power falls by almost half. The difference is the cube hiding in the fan laws.

These are the fan affinity laws for changing the speed of the same fan: Q2/Q1 = N2/N1, Δp2/Δp1 = (N2/N1)², and P2/P1 = (N2/N1)³. The page holds fan diameter, air density and efficiency constant and reports shaft power, not exact electrical input. Real motors, drives, filters, dampers and systems depart from the ideal — especially at very low speed — so measured manufacturer curves remain the authority. The cube-law result is still why variable-speed control can save so much energy: the U.S. Department of Energy gives the same 20%-speed-reduction example, while ANSI/AMCA 210 defines the underlying similarity relationships.
