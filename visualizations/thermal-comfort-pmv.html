The Fanger PMV/PPD model (ISO 7730, adopted by ASHRAE 55) puts a full body heat balance — metabolic heat in, against respiration, convection, radiation and sweat losses out — onto a −3…+3 comfort scale, then converts that into PPD, the percentage of people predicted to be dissatisfied (PPD = 100 − 95·e−0.03353·PMV4−0.2179·PMV2, never zero — nobody makes everyone happy). A room is compliant when −0.5 ≤ PMV ≤ +0.5 and PPD ≤ 10%, shown as the shaded band on the gauge above. Every exterior surface's temperature is calculated, not set directly. Steady-state conduction gives Tsurface = Tin − (Tin − Tout)·Rsi/Rtotal, where Rsi = 0.12 m²·K/W is the standard interior air-film resistance and Rtotal adds that surface's own R-value (entered in the imperial units common on Canadian insulation batts, converted internally: 1 = 0.176 m²·K/W) plus a 0.03 m²·K/W exterior air film — the same formula for a wall marked "Exterior" and for a ceiling switched to "Roof", just with that surface's own R-value. Windows use their U-value directly, since a U-factor already is the whole-assembly figure: heat flux is U·(Tin−Tout), and the same Rsi fraction of that gives the glass temperature. Window size deliberately does not appear in either formula — a bigger pane isn't colder glass, it's just more of it. What size (and which walls are exterior at all) actually changes: how much of the room's radiant "view" is glazing versus opaque wall versus roof (feeding tr below), and what share of the whole envelope's heat loss the windows carry, both shown in the readout under the gauge. Mean radiant temperature (tr) is a weighted average of every wall, window, ceiling and floor temperature, weighted by how much of each you can actually "see" from where you're standing (angle factor) — this page uses a simplified, illustrative angle-factor model based only on distance to each of the four walls, not full solid-angle geometry, but the direction is honest: stand in a corner where two walls are both exterior and both pull tr down at once, which is exactly why corners of old houses feel colder than a single-exterior-wall room even at the same air temperature. Operative temperature is approximated as the simple average of air and radiant temperature, valid when air speed is low. Clothing (clo) and activity (met) values are standard reference figures from ASHRAE's clothing-insulation tables and metabolic-rate tables, not measurements of any specific outfit or person. The draft, floor-temperature and radiant-asymmetry flags below the gauge are simplified proxies for ASHRAE 55's separate local-discomfort limits (draft rate, floor 19–29°C, radiant asymmetry <5°C for a cool surface) — real assessments measure them directly rather than approximating from the inputs here, and this page can flag more than one wall's asymmetry at once, which a single-number PMV never would.
