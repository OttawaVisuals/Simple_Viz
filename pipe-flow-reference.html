Pipe flow: which formula applies.

A working decision tree for choosing between Hagen–Poiseuille, Darcy–Weisbach, and compressible pipe-flow models — written after fixing a real bug in straw-hose-flow.html that came from using the wrong one. Meant to be checked before building or reviewing any page that pushes a fluid through a tube.

The decision tree

Three questions, in order. Skipping the first two and jumping straight to Hagen–Poiseuille because it's the simplest formula is exactly the mistake that shipped in straw-hose-flow.html for water at narrow diameters.

1
Is the fluid compressible in this context?
Liquid (water, milkshake, honey, …) — always incompressible at the pressures this site deals with. Go to question 2.
Gas (air) — incompressible only if the pressure drop is small relative to absolute pressure (ΔP << Patm) and the exit speed stays well under Mach 0.3. If either of those fails, skip questions 2–3 and go straight to the compressible section below.
2
What's the Reynolds number? — Re = ρvD/μ (equivalently Re = 4ρQ/(πμD) from flow rate).
Re ≲ 2,300 — laminar. Use Hagen–Poiseuille.
Re ≳ 4,000 — turbulent. Use Darcy–Weisbach with a turbulent friction factor.
2,300 – 4,000 — transitional. No exact theory exists here (real transition is intermittent, not a clean function of Re); an interpolated blend between the two is a reasonable engineering approximation, not physics.
3
Did question 1 send you to the compressible branch? Use the isothermal compressible pipe-flow equation instead of Darcy–Weisbach — but check the exit Mach number against the model's own limit before trusting the result (see below). Past that limit, only full Fanno flow is valid, and this site hasn't implemented it yet.

The formulas

Hagen–Poiseuille — laminar, incompressible

ΔP = 8ηLQ/(πr⁴) = 128ηLQ/(πD⁴)
The exact analytic solution for slow, steady, fully-developed laminar flow of a Newtonian fluid in a round tube. Valid only when: Re ≲ 2,300, fluid is incompressible, flow is fully developed (entrance length small vs. tube length). It is not a general-purpose "flow through a tube" formula — it's the laminar special case.

Darcy–Weisbach — general, incompressible

ΔP = f(L/D)(ρv²/2)
The general incompressible pipe-friction formula. Hagen–Poiseuille is the special case where f = 64/Re — substituting that in and simplifying reproduces the Hagen–Poiseuille formula exactly, so the two aren't competing equations, one is a special case of the other. For turbulent flow, f comes from an empirical correlation instead:
  • Laminar (Re≤2,300): f = 64/Re
  • Turbulent, smooth pipe (Re≥4,000): Haaland's explicit approximation to Colebrook, 1/√f = −1.8·log₁₀(6.9/Re)
  • Transitional (2,300–4,000): smoothstep-blended between the two — an interpolation, not a derivation
This is what straw-hose-flow.html's liquid branch now uses (fixed 2026-08-18) instead of a bare Hagen–Poiseuille call.

Isothermal compressible pipe flow — gases, meaningful ΔP

p₁² − p₂² = f(L/D)(ṁ/A)²RT
Combines the Darcy–Weisbach friction relation with the ideal gas law and continuity, integrated along the pipe assuming constant temperature (reasonable at the speeds involved here, since the tube has time to exchange heat with its surroundings). Needed once a gas's pressure drop stops being a small fraction of absolute pressure — which happens fast for anything straw-scale, since narrow tubes need real pressure to push meaningful airflow through them. This is what straw-hose-flow-darcy.html implements for air.
Known limit, not yet enforced in code: isothermal pipe flow chokes at Ma = 1/√γ ≈ 0.845 for air (γ=1.4) — not at Ma=1 like the more familiar adiabatic case. Past that point no isothermal solution exists for the given boundary pressures, and the equation above will still happily return a number (checked 2026-08-18: short length + wide diameter + max pressure on straw-hose-flow-darcy.html returns Mach 1.49, which is not a physically possible isothermal-flow result). The page currently only flags "Mach ≥0.3 — use full Fanno flow," which undersells this: past 0.845 the number isn't just imprecise, it doesn't correspond to any real flow. Open fix, pinned: detect and flag choking at Ma ≈ 0.845 explicitly, rather than reporting a number past it.

Fanno flow — adiabatic compressible, high Mach

Not implemented on this site yet.
The correct model once flow is fast enough, or the tube well-enough insulated, that the isothermal assumption itself breaks down (heat can't be exchanged fast enough to hold temperature constant). Chokes at Ma=1, the more familiar sonic limit. Needed for the high-Mach corner of the straw/hose problem that isothermal flow can't reach a valid answer for — see the open item above. Reference: NASA Fanno flow notes.

Rule of thumb for this site: viscous liquids (honey, milkshake) stay laminar almost no matter what you do to them within reasonable slider ranges — their high viscosity keeps Re tiny. Water and air are the ones worth checking by hand whenever a slider range changes, since both can plausibly cross into turbulent (or, for air, compressible) territory within numbers a visitor would actually pick.

Sources: Hagen–Poiseuille equation · Darcy–Weisbach equation · Reynolds number · NIST TN 2294 (isothermal compressible pipe flow, cited on the Darcy variant page) · NASA Fanno flow notes. The isothermal choking limit (Ma=1/√γ) is standard compressible-flow theory, covered in the same NIST note and any introductory gas-dynamics text (e.g. Shapiro, The Dynamics and Thermodynamics of Compressible Fluid Flow).
