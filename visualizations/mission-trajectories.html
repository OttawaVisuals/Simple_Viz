The camera changes, not the scale.

At launch, the view is only a few lunar distances wide. As Earth and the probe separate, the camera widens just enough to keep both visible. That is why the Sun and then the planets gradually enter the scene; the labelled scale bar is always in real units.

It is a trajectory, not live navigation.

The departure sequence uses daily JPL samples for each mission’s first 120 days; later points are 30 days apart. The connecting line is visual interpolation. Dates after today are predictions, and spacecraft files can be revised.

Data: NASA/JPL Horizons heliocentric geometric vectors for Voyager 1, Voyager 2, New Horizons, Juno, Parker Solar Probe, Cassini, Dawn, Earth, Moon, and the planets shown. The Earth distance is calculated from the difference between spacecraft and Earth vectors. Download the dataset. See JPL’s trajectory limitations before treating a spacecraft prediction as operational data.
