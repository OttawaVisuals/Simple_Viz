Why this window is fogging up

Air can carry invisible water vapour. When it touches glass colder than its dew point, that vapour has nowhere to stay gaseous.

Dew point is the temperature at which air reaches 100% relative humidity when cooled without changing its water-vapour content. This page uses the commonly used Magnus approximation over liquid water: γ = ln(RH/100) + aT/(b+T), then Td = bγ/(a−γ), with a = 17.62 and b = 243.12°C. The glass fogs in this model whenever its inside surface is at or below the calculated dew point. Real windows have surface-temperature variation, air movement and frames/edges that can be colder than the centre pane; these are omitted. See the US National Weather Service vapour-pressure reference for the underlying saturation-vapour-pressure relation.
