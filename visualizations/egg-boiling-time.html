Model, assumptions and sources

45°C and 35°C are comparison temperatures, not handling-safety or food-safety limits. The centre and boundary are different locations.

The equation above is a first-term estimate. The results, graph and animation use the full diffusion solution below. The enlarged spherical cross-section shows temperature by colour. Heat stored in the outer layers can keep warming the interior briefly after the move to the tap.

This is a homogeneous sphere with constant thermal properties. Its surface is held at the bath temperature: convection and shell resistance are not solved separately. The surface changes instantly when the bath changes; every interior point keeps its temperature continuously. The animation shows that ideal boundary condition, not a measured shell-cooling rate.

The model uses ρ = 1.038 g/cm³, cp = 3.7 J/(g·°C), k = 0.0054 W/(cm·°C), α = k/(ρcp), and R = (3M/(4πρ))1/3. These representative albumen constants are reported in the egg slide-rule notes citing Williams. All masses, including very large eggs, use the same ideal material.

With x = r/R and Fo = αt/R², the unit-temperature response is:

Θ(x,Fo) = 2 Σn=1…∞ (−1)n+1 sin(nπx)/(nπx) · exp(−n²π²Fo)

The centre uses the limit sin(nπx)/(nπx) → 1. A mathematically equivalent short-time image solution avoids early-time ringing. Heating is T = Th + (T₀ − Th)Θ. After moving to the tap at tb:

T = Tc + (T₀ − Th)Θ(x,αt/R²) + (Th − Tc)Θ(x,α(t−tb)/R²)

The target is evaluated at x = 0.691, an approximate yolk–white boundary. Boiling ends when that point reaches the chosen target; cooling thresholds are solved from the same continuing solution. The centre may remain cooler than the selected target. Doneness labels are approximate: coagulation takes time and this model does not predict food safety.

Williams' egg-boiling explanation motivates the model; its familiar 0.76 prefactor is the approximate first term at the yolk boundary. This page uses the fuller solution instead of reusing that approximation for early cooling. See the sphere solution (equations 8–10, fixed-surface limit).

Real eggs have different white/yolk properties, a shell, an air pocket and a nonspherical shape. The model omits those, changes during cooking and any warming of the cooling bath. The altitude option changes only boiling temperature. Predictions are illustrative, especially for very large eggs.
