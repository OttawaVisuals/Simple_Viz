This experimental page uses a low-Mach, isothermal, compressible form of the Darcy–Weisbach pressure-loss model. Air is treated as an ideal gas at 20°C in a smooth, straight, round tube. The Darcy friction factor is recalculated from Reynolds number: f = 64/Re for laminar flow, a smooth-pipe turbulent correlation above Re = 4,000, and a blend through the uncertain transition region. The calculation starts with atmospheric pressure plus the selected mouth pressure and returns airflow at the open end.

The 20 kPa upper setting is an approximate maximal blowing pressure drawn from clinical mouth-pressure measurements, not something everyone can sustain. Entrance losses, leaks, bends, joints, and kinked straws are omitted, so real chained straws generally perform worse. Results at or above Mach 0.3 are flagged because density and acceleration changes then require a full constant-area compressible treatment such as Fanno flow. This page models air only; milkshakes need measured non-Newtonian rheology.
