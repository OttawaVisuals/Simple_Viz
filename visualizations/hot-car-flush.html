These bars explain the flow path; they are not measured cooling rates for a particular car.

This is an air-flushing technique, not a complete cooling model. Opening one rear window and swinging the opposite front door creates a pressure-driven exchange path. Geometry, door travel, wind and vehicle design all matter, so this page does not claim a universal number of air changes or degrees cooled. Cross-ventilation patterns in vehicle cabins are supported by published vehicle-airflow simulations; measured stationary-vehicle exchange rates also rise markedly when windows or fresh-air ventilation are used. Surfaces warmed in the sun cool much more slowly than the cabin air, so use the AC safely after flushing and never leave children or pets in a parked car.
